## T1. END OF SEASON / DIAPAUSE / HOST AVAILABILITY (insects, mites)

### Lobesia botrana (ευδεμίδα)
- Diapause induction: Type I (long-day) response; determining factor is scotophase > 11 h (i.e. day length < 13 h) experienced by eggs/larvae, regardless of photophase; under LD 12:12 almost 100 % diapause at 12–30 °C; intensity (pupal duration) rises with temperature. [ABS] Roditakis & Karandinos 2001, Physiol. Entomol. 26:329–340 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/32c771296424557f8f287f78f0c51bdb/?utm_source=claude_code . Roditakis 1993 PhD thesis (Greek, Crete): critical scotophase 11 h [SEC] — https://www.openarchives.gr/aggregator-openarchives/edm/phdtheses/000040-10442_2483
- Decreasing photoperiod also makes 2nd-gen larvae go through 6 instars (slower) in cooler NE Italy; in warmer areas 5 instars → economically important 3rd generation. [ABS] Pavan et al. 2013 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/95f630ba480a551bb54bd61fbe355b4f/?utm_source=claude_code
- Overwintering model (pre-diapause/diapause/post-diapause, photoperiod-driven) parametrised with data incl. Cyprus. [ABS] Baumgärtner et al. 2012 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/35cf64ba02c35be39ba61526c79faf90/?utm_source=claude_code
- CRETE FLIGHTS, Heraklion warning service, Αμπέλι Δελτίο Νο 15, 12-07-2024 [EXT, PDF read]: https://minagric.gr/images/stories/docs/nea/GEORGPROEIDOPOIHSEIS/HRAKLEIO/2024/ampeli_120724.pdf
  - ΠΕ Ηρακλείου–Λασιθίου: start 2nd flight 7–8 May (early zone), 10–11 May (intermediate), 13–16 May (mid-early), 19–20 May (late); start 3rd flight 25–27 June / 28–29 June / 2–4 July / 11–12 July. ΠΕ Χανίων–Ρεθύμνου: 2nd flight 11–12 May / 17–19 May / 24–26 May; 3rd flight 29–30 June / 10–11 July.
  - Recommended spray starts (3rd flight = 2nd fruit generation): 2–4 July (early) … 17–18 July (late); repeat 13–14 / 15–17 July. "Tolerance of bunch infestation must be very small to zero." Applications "from dusk onwards" (heat, wind). "Repeat dates are indicative — take into account the duration of action of the product used in the previous spray."
  - Sultanina phenology 12 July 2024: early zone "grapes ripening" (BBCH 85), mid-early veraison completed (81), late zone veraison. 2024 was >2 weeks earlier than 2023; 2025 followed 2024 with a 4-day delay [SEC, search summary of Heraklion phenology review].
  - A 16-page Heraklion three-year vine review exists (ampeli_trietia_290126_.pdf) but could not be rendered (no poppler) — https://www.minagric.gr/images/stories/docs/nea/GEORGPROEIDOPOIHSEIS/HRAKLEIO/ampeli_trietia_290126_.pdf
- Day length 13 h at ~35 °N is reached around the third week of August (astronomical computation, not from a source) → larvae hatching from late August onwards in Crete are diapause-destined; combined with harvest of sultanina (July–Aug, early zones) and wine grapes (Aug–Sept), risk to fruit ends at harvest.
- WHAT AN OPERATIONAL RULE COULD LOOK LIKE: (a) stop fruit-damage alerts at the field's declared harvest date (calendar entry) — no host; (b) independent of DD, when day length < 13 h (Crete ≈ 20–25 Aug) the generation in progress is the last one that matters; downgrade to "monitoring only" after its larval window (≈ 3 weeks) closes; (c) never show "full generation activity" after harvest or after 30 Sept; (d) winter: pupae in diapause → "no risk" until the first spring flight (Heraklion 1st flight is in April; 2nd flight early May).
- RISK: in late-harvest wine vineyards (mountain zones, Sept–Oct) a 4th partial flight can still oviposit on bunches — keep alerts until harvest, not until a fixed date.

### Prays oleae (πυρηνοτρήτης)
- Three generations: flower (anthophagous, spring, larvae in flowers), fruit (carpophagous, summer, larvae in young fruit/seed), leaf (phyllophagous, autumn–winter in leaves; "usually does little harm"). [SEC] Acta Hort 2002 summary; Trás-os-Montes thresholds (flower generation): treat at 4.0 % / 6.0 % flower infestation in intermediate/high-yield years, 8.0 % / 11.0 % in low-yield years [ABS] — https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65de274e0f3e94b9e5cb290c
- Heraklion Δελτίο Ελιάς Νο 4, 4 May 2023 [EXT, PDF read]: https://www.minagric.gr/images/stories/docs/nea/GEORGPROEIDOPOIHSEIS/HRAKLEIO/2023/elia_040523.pdf — anthophagous adults rising in mid-early zones at flowering (stage E–F); treat at start of flowering ONLY in groves with moderate/poor bloom ("5 % of flowers suffice for a good crop"); organic: Bt when 5–25 % of flowers open; bulletins issued per zone (early/coastal, mid/inland, late/mountain).
- Spain (RAIF/Junta) [SEC]: anthophagous threshold ≥ 5 % inflorescences attacked (2025 article), carpophagous "> 20 % of fruits with eggs" (Vida Rural 2008) — https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/alerta-jaen-polilla-olivo ; https://mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/biblioteca/revistas/pdf_Vrural/Vrural_2008_276_44_48.pdf
- Months (Crete bulletins): anthophagous flight late April–May; carpophagous eggs on young fruit June (fruit-set); phyllophagous autumn — no treatment. [EXT/SEC]
- RULE: windows tied to phenology, not DD-to-31-Dec: anthophagous = from stage D (inflorescence swelling) to end of flowering; carpophagous = fruit set to pit hardening (June–early July; larvae exit at pit hardening → "σκλήρυνση πυρήνα"); phyllophagous = information only, no spray. Outside these windows show "no actionable risk".
- RISK: fruit-generation damage is decided in June; missing the egg window cannot be recovered later.

### Bactrocera oleae (δάκος)
- Samos (Greece): bimodal, first peak early July, larger peak late September; hours > 32 °C key negative driver; bait sprays less effective in autumn. [ABS] J. Appl. Entomol. 2011 135:359–366 — https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Journal_of_Applied_Entomology_(2011)_135,_359-366
- Crete organic orchard: summer activity crash when mean daily maximum > ~31 °C [SEC]; lab: no egg hatch at highs of 35–37.8 °C (Wang et al. 2009 Environ. Entomol. 38:1496) [ABS] — https://academic.oup.com/ee/article/38/5/1496/410338
- Females reproductively immature in spring/early summer (Greece, Italy); late-summer ovarian maturation favoured by fruit presence, inhibited by low RH / high T [SEC].
- Spain RAIF Málaga, 14–18 Oct 2024 [EXT, PDF read]: https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/agriculturapescaaguaydesarrollorural/raif/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Informe_Fitosanitario_1014_1020-6.pdf — provincial means: 4.5 % fruit stung, 1.7 % with live stages, 0.4 % exit holes; yellow pheromone plates 3.2 adults/plate/day (Ronda 21.4), McPhail 2.1 flies/trap/day; 72 % of females fertile; "T > 36 °C with low RH strongly limits viability; optimum 20–25 °C"; Integrated Production tolerance differs oil vs table olives (any stung fruit unacceptable for table).
- Crete dakoktonia announcements (crete.gov.gr 2025–2026) only state "spraying starts if suitable weather"; no numeric trap thresholds found online [SEC].
- Heraklion, 30 Jan 2020 [EXT]: the 2019 crop loss was mainly autumn olive-fly outbreak under prolonged favourable T/RH; mummified fruit confused with anthracnose.
- RULE: active season = from fruit receptivity (pit hardening, ~late June/July) to harvest; suppress alerts (a) when mean daily max > 31–32 °C for several consecutive days (summer arrest, show "heat-suppressed"), (b) after harvest/fruit removal; autumn (Sept–Nov, 20–25 °C, rising RH) = highest risk until harvest. Use trap captures and % stung fruit (RAIF-style), not DD.
- RISK: unharvested groves keep overwintering populations (Heraklion 2020 note) — a late-harvest neighbour can seed next year's spring population.

### Rhynchites cribripennis (ρυγχίτης)
- Zakynthos: adults build up on trees May–June, peaks mid-June 1994 (19.9 adults/twig) and early July 1995 (7.7/twig). [ABS] J. Econ. Entomol. 2004 97:316–320 — https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-economic-entomology/volume-97/issue-2/0022-0493-97.2.316/Rates-of-Population-Increase-Abundance-and-Life-Stage-Distribution-of/10.1603/0022-0493-97.2.316.full
- Crete bulletin practice (via secondary): beat branches over a white sheet early morning; treat (combined with Prays carpophagous spray) when > 3–4 adults per branch [SEC] — https://wikifarmer.com/library/en/article/olive-pests-greece-june-2026
- RULE: adult window late April–July (one generation, larva in the stone, overwinters in soil); alerts only May–July, and only for groves where beating finds > 3–4 adults/branch; no risk after pit hardening/August.
- RISK: threshold is from bulletin practice, not a validated economic threshold.

### Saissetia oleae (λεκάνιο) & Parlatoria oleae (παρλατόρια)
- UC IPM black scale [EXT]: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/olive/black-scale/ — honeydew scouting April & October (25–30 trees); May adult count (10 branches × 10 trees): light < 1 adult/branch, moderate 1–4, heavy 4–10, severe > 10; egg hatch mid-July; treat crawlers 15 July–harvest, severe cases after hatch but before August; one generation/yr inland, several on the coast.
- Spain: population synchronous, first instars predominate end of July → best spray timing [ABS] — https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/647247e4e17b74d2224f6db9
- Parlatoria: northern Israel DD model — spring oviposition at 40.1 DD, egg hatch at 184.8 DD (base not shown in summary) [ABS] Environ. Entomol. 1996 25:1–6 — https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Environmental_Entomology_(1996)_25,_1-6 ; 2–3 overlapping generations/yr on olive (Egypt) [SEC].
- Heraklion 4 May 2023 [EXT]: paraffinic oil when new shoots are 6–8 cm (3–4 leaf pairs) also controls "young nymphal stages of scales (pollinia, parlatoria, aspidiotus) and psylla".
- RULE: scale alerts are crawler-window alerts: Saissetia = July–Aug (plus a coastal autumn window); Parlatoria = spring (May) and summer; outside crawler windows "treatment not effective — monitor". No numeric crawlers/leaf threshold found in Spanish/Greek sources (not found).
- RISK: pruning/ventilation is the main control; late-summer oil restrictions for table olives (UC: no oil 20 Aug–harvest).

### Palpita unionalis (μαργαρόνια)
- Heraklion 4 May 2023 [EXT]: spring-generation larvae destroy tender new growth; protect nurseries, young plantations and severely renewal-pruned trees; control checks only on those. 4–5 generations/yr in Greece [SEC] — https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Palpita_vitrealis
- RULE: risk only where there is tender growth (young/pruned/irrigated trees), spring flush and autumn flush; adult bearing groves → "no actionable risk".

### Euphyllura (βαμβακάδα)
- Heraklion 4 May 2023 [EXT]: adults from mild autumn/winter oviposit on new growth "until end of May"; rising temperature and dry heat halt activity; treat only if poor bloom AND > 30 % of inflorescences infested, preferably paraffinic oil.
- RULE: alert window March–May; stop at end of May / when daily max > ~30 °C.

### Ceratitis capitata
- Heraklion, Crete, 3-yr winter study [ABS] Ann. Entomol. Soc. Am. 1996 89:526–534 — https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Annals_of_the_Entomological_Society_of_America_(1996)_89,_526-534 : breeding continues Nov–May at reduced rate; pupae formed Dec–Feb need ≈ 2 months; infested oranges found all winter; larval mortality 40–70 %. Lower development threshold 10.2 °C (Duyck & Quilici 2002) [SEC]. Spain Integrated Production trap threshold "0.5" (flies/trap/day, Málaga citrus bulletin) [SEC].
- RULE: no diapause in Crete — alerts follow host availability (citrus Oct–May, stone fruit/figs summer); use trap counts (≥ 0.5 flies/trap/day as RAIF reference) rather than DD-to-31-Dec; show reduced winter rate when mean T < 12 °C.

### Tuta absoluta
- Lower threshold 8.0 °C, 460 DD/generation (CLIMEX parameter table) [SEC] — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4529269/table/pone.0135283.t001 ; 7.78 °C (USDA white paper) [SEC]; facultative pupal diapause after larvae at 2–5 °C and short days, θL = 5.37 °C [ABS] J. Pest Sci. 2021 — https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-03275941
- Almería/RAIF: reports captures in nearly all tomato plots and plant damage "1 in 20 plants"; numeric trap thresholds not found online (not found).
- RULE: greenhouse tomato = year-round; no calendar stop; alert on pheromone trap trend + plant damage; slow down (longer generation) when GH mean T < 10 °C.

### Bemisia tabaci
- Lower thresholds: 10.2 °C (Q biotype on tomato, Bonato 2007), 11.53 °C / 307 DD (Caciagli 1998), 9.3 °C / 485 DD (Sicily, biotype T) [ABS]. RAIF pepper bulletin Sept 2024: present in all greenhouses, 1 in 25 plants [SEC]. Thresholds per leaf: not found.
- RULE: year-round in plastic houses; TYLCV vector → alert is presence-based; winter development slows below ~10–11 °C.

### Myzus persicae — UC IPM lower threshold 4.0 °C, upper 30 °C [EXT] https://ipm.ucanr.edu/weather/phenology-models-description/green-peach-aphid/ ; anholocyclic on Crete (mild winters) [SEC]. RULE: no winter stop; alert when colonies found (scouting), not DD.
### Tetranychus urticae — critical night length 10–11 h at 16–19 °C, 12 h at 22 °C and induction < 100 % at 22 °C [ABS]; critical photoperiod ca 13.5 h L:D (Japan) [ABS]. Lower development threshold: not found in this session. RULE: outdoor crops — diapause from ~Sept (night > 11 h + cool); greenhouses (warm, lit) → no reliable diapause, keep alerts year-round; mite alerts should be scouting-driven.
### Leptinotarsa decemlineata — de Wilde 1959 photoperiod control of adult diapause; northern populations have longer critical photoperiod (15 h cited for Petroskoi); short-day plants induce > 65 % diapause [SEC]. Mediterranean value: not found. RULE: 1–2 generations; after mid-August adults enter soil → stop alerts; potato harvest ends host.
### Phthorimaea operculella — 4.9 °C / 741.7 DD (one lab study) [SEC]; no diapause; RULE: risk tied to tubers/host presence, storage; stop at harvest.
### Thrips tabaci — not found in this session.
### Cydia pomonella — critical photoperiod (50 % diapause) 15:9 at 17 °C, 14.4:9.6 at 22 °C, 13.8:10.2 at 27 °C, 13.44:10.56 at 30 °C (Garcia-Salazar et al. 1988 Environ. Entomol. 17:626) [ABS] — https://academic.oup.com/ee/article/17/4/626/407589 ; Durango (Mexico) induction 13 Aug–4 Oct at 13.1–11.9 light-h [SEC]. RULE: larvae after ~mid-August (day < 13.5–14 h) diapause; stop fruit alerts at harvest.
### Phyllocnistis citrella — Valencia mature trees: damage July–Nov; 52 % vs 8 % of summer–autumn flush leaf area damaged unsprayed vs sprayed; spring flush not affected [ABS] J. Econ. Entomol. 2002 95:966 — https://wiki.pestinfo.org/wiki/Journal_of_Economic_Entomology_(2002)_95,_966-974 ; EPPO 1995: protect spring/autumn (sometimes summer) flushes, mostly nurseries/young trees [SEC]. RULE: alert only when a flush is present AND tree is young/nursery; mature trees = no spray.
### Panonychus citri / ulmi — Spain thresholds found only for T. urticae on citrus (> 10 % leaves occupied, GVA 2016) [SEC]; P. citri numbers not found.

T1 overall RULE: replace "high risk until 31 Dec" with a per-pest season mask = {host window (phenology/harvest), diapause cue (day length < X h and/or T), heat arrest (olive fly)}; outside the mask show "out of season — monitoring only". RISK: masks must be per crop/zone (early/mid/late zones differ by 2–3 weeks in Crete) and must respect the field's actual harvest date.

## T2. FUNGICIDE PROTECTION PERIOD IN DSS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- VitiMeteo-Plasmopara (Agroscope/WBI Freiburg) [FT] Bleyer et al. 2020, PMC7355483 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7355483 : protection of one spray is maintained while new leaf area per primary shoot stays < 300–400 cm² (≈ 2–3 new leaves); above that, if a weak infection is forecast → re-spray "after 80 % of the pathogen incubation time has passed but as early as possible before rain"; strong infection → curative at next possible date; if no infection predicted, interval extended "until the next critical event (rain or prolonged leaf wetness)". Trials: 600 cm² interval possible with potassium phosphonate added; 800 cm² insufficient; after BBCH 73 use number of new leaves.
- vite.net / Horta: fungicide persistence model (Caffi & Rossi 2018, Phytopathol. Mediterr.) merged in vite.net; residual efficacy from product characteristics, weather, disease development, phenological growth [SEC] — https://www.bio-conferences.org/articles/bioconf/pdf/2022/09/bioconf_gdpm2022_03002.pdf (403 on fetch); folpet persisted up to 80 mm artificial rain then dropped sharply [SEC]. Parameters not accessible.
- RIMpro: cover = growth-dilution model + rain wash-off, 7-day forecast; no public mm coefficients [SEC] — https://www.treefruit.com.au/orchard/crop-protection/disease/992-rimpro-supports-effective-blackspot-management-part-2
- Rain wash-off numbers (extension) [EXT]: Penn State https://extension.psu.edu/fungicides-and-rain — ~50 % of captan residue removed by ~1 mm rain (MSU), little more after; 0.1 inch (2.5 mm) removes 20–25 % of ziram/mancozeb on grape/blueberry, control loss detectable after 1–2 inches (25–50 mm); week-old residues below effective level after 0.5 inch (12 mm); rule of thumb 1 inch ≈ half residue, ≥ 2 inches most; ≥ 4 h drying. Grape pathology ext. https://ext.grapepathology.org/rains-and-fungicide-sprays — "2 inches of rain or 2 weeks, whichever first" (not valid during rapid shoot growth); 1.5–2 h drying; sharp drop after 0.04 inch then plateau (rate >> minimum effective dose). Copper on apple: 0.2 inch removed 80 % [SEC] https://viticulture.uga.edu/?p=298
- Interval-based systems: TOM-CAST (UConn) [EXT] https://ipm.cahnr.uconn.edu/using-forecasting-models-to-time-tomato-fungicides-and-insect-management — re-spray every 20 DSV (chlorothalonil/strobilurin) or 15 DSV (mancozeb); max 14 days; FAST: CS7 ≥ 13 and/or CR5 ≥ 9, variant with max 14-day interval [SEC] https://www.plantmanagementnetwork.org/pub/trial/fntests/reports/2002/V114.pdf ; SimCast: spray when blight units OR fungicide units threshold reached first; 30 BU for susceptible cultivar [SEC]; the exact FU-by-rainfall/day table (Fry et al. 1983, Grünwald et al. 2002 Plant Dis. 86:1163) could not be retrieved (APS PDFs 403). TomatoPM assumes one spray protects 10 days [EXT] https://ipm.ucanr.edu/MODELS/TOMPM/abouttomatopm.html
- Label/legal: Reg. (EC) 1107/2009 Art. 55 — proper use = compliance with the conditions of Art. 31 "specified on the labelling" + IPM principles [EXT] https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2009/1107/article/55/data.html ; Reg. (EU) 547/2011 Annex I lists label elements incl. authorisation conditions [SEC] https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2011/547/2013-07-01/eng/pdf . No EPPO/EU text found that explicitly addresses DSS advice vs. label interval (not found) — but a re-application earlier than the label's minimum interval is a non-label use under Art. 55. Heraklion vine bulletin [EXT]: "repeat dates are indicative… take into account the duration of action of the product"; olive bulletin [EXT]: copper = protectant, keep ≥ 21-day PHI.
- RULE: protection = min(label interval, growth-dilution, rain-wash-off) with a step/plateau shape, not exponential: full protection for the label interval unless (i) cumulative rain since application ≥ 25 mm (contact) → protection lost (copper/mancozeb: flag at ≥ 10–12 mm if residue > 7 days old), (ii) new growth > 2–3 leaves/shoot (vine) or equivalent flush → "new growth unprotected" (systemic/translaminar products exempt within label), (iii) after label interval expires → unprotected. Never display "spray now" before the label minimum interval has elapsed; show "label interval not yet elapsed — next earliest date X".
- RISK: the exponential decay reaching 0.3 at 53 % of the label period produces re-spray advice inside the label interval (illegal use + resistance); rain-wash thresholds are product-specific and the only ones found are US extension rules of thumb.

## T3. CUCUMBER DOWNY MILDEW IN PLASTIC GREENHOUSES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Yang, Li, Zhao et al. 2007 NZ J. Agric. Res. (unheated greenhouses, Beijing) [ABS] https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00288230709510411 : infection thresholds = daily temperature range ≤ 5 °C, daily mean RH ≥ 80 %, daily mean T 15–25 °C (autumn); symptom appearance rate = reciprocal of incubation days fitted by a cubic polynomial of temperature.
- Zhao, Li, Yang, Sun 2011 Comput. Electron. Agric. (EWMPICDW) [ABS] https://consensus.app/papers/details/13093c25f5ca5b8a87d0a27a65294723/?utm_source=claude_code : LWD estimated by leaf-wetness sensor + RH-threshold model; infection sub-model = threshold on LWD × mean T during wetness; incubation sub-model = nonlinear regression on T; warnings > 2 d before symptoms; positive warning raises P(disease) 0.68 → 0.96. Numeric thresholds in full text (not accessed).
- Li Ming et al. 2010 (Chinese) [ABS]: RH threshold for "leaf wet" calibrated at 89–93 % (three methods), LWD error 1–2 h. Wen et al. 2019 [ABS]: first symptoms 7.00 d after inoculation with 4 h leaf wetness, 3.25 d with 12 h wetness; longer wetness → shorter incubation.
- Wang Hui et al. 2015 (canopy T/RH model + primary infection & latent-period models, validated Sept–Oct 2014) [ABS] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26995910/ ; Liu et al. 2022 Comput. Electron. Agric.: 72-h greenhouse climate forecast coupled to disease model, first alert matched first infection 24 Apr 2021 [ABS].
- Sporulation (Sun et al. 2017, sporulation part only) [ABS]: lesions sporulate 4 h after wetting in darkness; plenty at RH 100 %, few at 90 %.
- India (Ghosh et al., J. Agrometeorol.) [ABS]: initiation when RH > 94 %, mean T 24–30 °C, leaf wetness ≥ 8 h; night LWD/night RH > 95 % cumulative predictors.
- Israel (Cohen/Rotem/Shtienberg greenhouse rules), Almería, Antalya, Greek rules: not found online in this session.
- Time of day of spraying: BC Ministry greenhouse tomato Botrytis [EXT] https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/agriculture-and-seafood/animal-and-crops/plant-health/disease-factsheets/greenhouse-vegetables/botrytis_canker_of_greenhouse_tomato.pdf : "Avoid spray operations in the late afternoons and on cloudy days; spray in the morning hours"; any water-based irrigation/spray in the morning on sunny days to minimise prolonged wetness; 2–3 h wetness suffice for Botrytis germination/penetration. Lettuce DM (Scherm/Koike) treat when leaf wetness ends ≥ 10:00 [SEC].
- RULE (plastic house, no heating): daily risk = [mean T 15–25 °C] AND [LWD ≥ 2–4 h (sensor or RH ≥ 90 % hours) at 15–25 °C] AND [daily T range ≤ 5 °C or RH ≥ 80 % daily mean] AND inoculum flag (symptoms seen in house or neighbouring houses); incubation countdown 3–7 d (shorter with longer wetness); advice: ventilate at dawn, avoid evening irrigation/spraying, spray mornings; "spray before next condensation night" if inoculum present.
- RISK: without an inoculum flag the weather rule fires every humid autumn night; RH sensors in canopy, not at the roof, are required (Chinese models are canopy-based).

## T4. INCUBATION / LATENT PERIODS BY TEMPERATURE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- P. cubensis: 3.25 d (12 h LWD) – 7.0 d (4 h LWD) [ABS Wen 2019]; sporulation 4 h after wetting [ABS Sun 2017]; cubic-polynomial T function [ABS Yang 2007]. Full table not accessed.
- Phytophthora infestans: APS simulation uses latent 6 / 6 / 7 d (S / MR / R cultivars) [EXT] https://www.scientificsocieties.org/aps/late_blight/Lateblight_Manual.html ; Brazil isolates latent 68–93 h (2.8–3.9 d) at 22 °C [SEC]; "3–5 d" trade [SEC].
- Plasmopara viticola: symptoms 4 d at 20/25 °C, 9 d at 10/15 °C; 4–21 d range [SEC] (Müller/Goidanich tables not retrieved; Goidanich index summarised as daily RH+T index to 100 % [SEC] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-agricultural-science (Spain 2019 paper)). Müller: no risk if mean T < 12 or > 28 °C [SEC]. Metos support page returned no text.
- Alternaria solani: 4 h wetness enough on susceptible hybrid, ≥ 6 h on cv. Rio Rojo; symptoms rise up to 24 h wetness; susceptibility increases with plant age [ABS] Vloutoglou et al. 2000 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/815a9b97dcba5ce5b2a5a835a277680d/?utm_source=claude_code ; incubation "5–7 d" trade [SEC]; sporulation optimum 25 °C / 90 % RH [ABS]. Greek tomato fruit rots (Thomidis 2023): optimum 20–30 °C, 24 h wetness [ABS].
- Podosphaera xanthii: optimum germination 24.4 °C, infection 25.7 °C, sporulation 22.3 °C; sporulation assessed 6–15 d [ABS] Trecate et al. 2019 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/ea67f937fcab5e719956793497e38a51/?utm_source=claude_code
- Leveillula taurica: pepper — disease development started 8 d after inoculation, max severity 15 d [ABS] Kim et al. 2009 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/6ed46ecc709a5808ad3e8186bf0b2a15/?utm_source=claude_code ; Guzmán-Plazola 2003 (latent-period models in full text, not accessed) — https://consensus.app/papers/details/faa052fdbb3f556e927dea86e101aff4/?utm_source=claude_code
- Botrytis cinerea tomato stems: infection 5–26 °C, fastest at 15 °C; only wounded stems; susceptibility 60 % → 8 % as wound ages 0 → 24 h; low VPD (< 0.2 kPa) prolongs wound susceptibility; sporulation none within 20 d at 5 or 26 °C [ABS] O'Neill et al. 1997 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/63227bb6501551c9974fc6d5108890e1/?utm_source=claude_code ; Eden 1996: raising T 15→25 °C reduces stem infection; lowering RH has small effect on stem infection [ABS].
- Spilocaea oleagina: minimum incubation 28 d (youngest leaves), many infections latent 6 months [ABS] Viruega 2011 — https://apsnet.org/publications/plantdisease/2011/September/Pages/95_9_1139.aspx ; "about 2 weeks, several weeks if hot season follows" (Metos) [EXT]; latent 256 – > 3000 DD (review) [SEC]; Roubal et al. 2013 Plant Pathol. 62:657 latent period vs air T after rain (not accessed).
- Colletotrichum (olive): infection 10–25 °C, shortest latent at 17–20 °C; latent period May–Oct, epidemic onset with ripening (early Nov), development Nov–Mar; 5 % / 20 % incidence at 1.0 h / 12.2 h wetness at optimum [ABS] Moral et al. 2012 ×2 — https://consensus.app/papers/details/ff467e22eee5526ca787a31f5027e2b6/?utm_source=claude_code ; https://consensus.app/papers/details/f2bb60bf0b0053e5bcea8b3da267a60d/?utm_source=claude_code
- RULE: use pathogen-specific incubation to (a) delay "symptoms expected" messages and (b) set the curative window (VitiMeteo-style 80 % of incubation); for Spilocaea and Colletotrichum the latent period is weeks–months, so symptom-based feedback is useless for timing.
- RISK: most values are growth-chamber, constant-temperature values.

## T5. LEAFHOPPERS (Empoasca decipiens, Asymmetrasca decedens)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Directly measured T0 for E. decipiens: not found. Raupach et al. 2002 Crop Prot. [ABS] https://consensus.app/papers/details/bf731e48c0075dffa151da42e581f92d/?utm_source=claude_code : larval development ~3× longer at 15 °C than ≥ 28 °C; shortest at 30 °C (10.2 d broad bean, 12.5 d aubergine); shortest egg stage at 35 °C; adults prefer older cucumber plants. Naseri 2008 (already known) gives field thermal constants only.
- Thresholds (Crete vine, Heraklion 12-07-2024) [EXT]: check 100 random leaves (underside, 1–2 per vine, mid-shoot to 2 leaves below tip); treat at 50–100 nymphs (άπτερα) per 100 leaves. Spain (RAIF Andalucía): treat against nymphs (most sensitive stage); adults low until mid-June then strongly rising in summer; A. decedens > 85 % of captures (Valencia peach/almond) [SEC] https://mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf%5Fplagas%2FBSVP%2D24%2D02%2D279%2D292%2Epdf ; no nymphs/leaf threshold for peach/almond found. US E. fabae: 1 nymph/leaf snap bean; 10 nymphs/105 leaves potato [SEC].
- Warning services (Crete, Andalucía) use scouting counts and yellow traps, not DD (no DD-based leafhopper warnings found).
- RULE: leafhopper alerts = scouting-triggered (≥ 0.5–1 nymph/leaf, i.e. 50–100 per 100 leaves) during June–September; DD only to estimate nymph timing after a trap peak; no alerts Nov–April.
- RISK: borrowed T0 (11.39 °C) for E. decipiens has no primary measurement.

## T6. OLIVE DISEASES — Spilocaea & Colletotrichum
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Heraklion Δελτίο Ελιάς Νο 10, 11 Oct 2023 [EXT, PDF read] https://www.minagric.gr/images/stories/docs/nea/GEORGPROEIDOPOIHSEIS/HRAKLEIO/2023/elia_111023.pdf : Kyklokonio — favoured by T drop to 16–20 °C and wet weather (dew/fog leaf wetting); control = preventive copper (or other approved) "before the start of the rains" in humid/dense groves and susceptible cvs (Throumbolia, Tsounati, Amfissis, Kalamon); Koroneiki relatively resistant; cultural: open pruning, drainage. Cercospora: immediate preventive spray with products also active on kyklokonio. Copper = protectant; ≥ 21-day PHI. Knot: copper after harvest/pruning/frost/hail.
- Heraklion 30 Jan 2020 [EXT, PDF read] https://www.minagric.gr/images/stories/docs/nea/GEORGPROEIDOPOIHSEIS/HRAKLEIO/enhmerotiko_elia300120.pdf : fungal infections on olive are treated "mainly in spring (protect new growth) and again in autumn"; winter = only copper for knot/lichens/sooty mould; Colletotrichum "because of its climatic requirements does not (yet) occur endemically on the island"; "mummified" dakos-damaged fruit mistaken for anthracnose.
- Spain RAIF Málaga Oct 2024 [EXT]: repilo favoured 8–24 °C, optimum 20 °C, RH optimum 100 %; strategy preventive "when environmental conditions are favourable"; monitoring = % leaves with incubated (NaOH test) and visible repilo (1.7 % / 0.7 % provincial mean); most groves receive ≥ 2 applications before autumn or spring rains [SEC Vida Rural]; Castilla-La Mancha: autumn treatment after rains + mild T where symptoms appear [SEC].
- Models: Viruega et al. 2011 Plant Dis. 95:1139 [ABS]: Analytis-beta, optimum 15.5 °C, minimum wetness 11.9 h; min incubation 28 d. Obanor (NZ, lab) min wetness 18/12/12/12/24 h at 5/10/15/20/25 °C [SEC]; conidial germination 5–25 °C optimum 20 °C, ≥ 12 h wetness [SEC]. Metos olive model [EXT] https://metos.global/en/disease-models-olive/ : germination 0–27 °C, optimum 16–21 °C; minimum wetness 24 h (5 °C), 12 h (10 °C), 9 h (15 °C), 9 h (20 °C), 12 h (25 °C); 6 h at 20 °C only if followed by 100 % RH; needs free water; RH 98 %; infection all year except hot dry summer. Greek evaluation of two forecasting models in N. Greece (Plants 2021, PMC8231239) — not readable (403).
- Anthracnose: Moral et al. 2012 (see T4) — T 10–25 °C, optimum 17–20 °C, wetness 1 h → 5 %, 12.2 h → 20 % incidence; latent May–Oct, epidemics only from ripening (Nov) and only in wet seasons; mummies in canopy keep producing conidia 6 months; Moral 2008/2009: susceptibility rises with fruit maturity [SEC].
- RULE: Spilocaea — season windows Oct–Apr (not summer: Metos/Viruega), infection event = wetness ≥ 9–12 h at 10–20 °C (≥ 24 h at 5 or 25 °C) with rain/dew; advice = protectant copper BEFORE forecast autumn rain and again late winter before spring rains; incubation ≥ 28 d so do not use "symptoms" for timing. Colletotrichum — no risk before fruit colour change; from veraison (Oct) to harvest, risk when wetness ≥ 12 h at 15–25 °C and rain; Crete: show as low-prior unless field has history (not endemic per PKPFP).
- RISK: current all-year "6 h / 10–25 °C / RH ≥ 70 %" rule fires in summer dew and in spring on unripe fruit; copper timing must respect 21-day PHI before harvest.

## T7. POWDERY MILDEWS OF GREENHOUSE VEGETABLES
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Leveillula taurica: Guzmán-Plazola et al. 2003 [ABS]: ≥ 30 °C deleterious; progress faster at 20 than 25 °C; RH 50–70 % optimal; 20–40 % reduces; 80–90 % favours germination but limits lesion growth; 2–3 daily exposures ≥ 2 h at 35 °C suppress disease 70–92 %. Egypt: severity + at 15–30 °C, RH 60–100 % [ABS]. UC TomatoPM model [EXT] https://ipm.ucanr.edu/MODELS/TOMPM/abouttomatopm.html : linear discriminant classifies each day (non-/moderately/conducive) from canopy T, RH, leaf wetness; 6-day window → low/moderate/severe; spray assumed to protect 10 days; two consecutive non-conducive days → 70–75 % fewer lesions; model under-predicts since 2007 [EXT] https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/tomato/powdery-mildew-on-field-grown-tomatoes/
- Podosphaera xanthii: UC IPM cucurbits [EXT] https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/cucurbits/powdery-mildew : "Apply a treatment when disease symptoms first occur and repeat if symptoms reappear"; RH 50–90 % favours; no numeric threshold; BC/OMAFRA: "no specific action threshold" [SEC]. Trecate 2019 optima (see T4). Sapak 2023: germination best 28 °C at saturation; declines with VPD [ABS].
- Oidium neolycopersici: no forecast model found (not found).
- Crete vine (Erysiphe necator, Heraklion 12-07-2024) [EXT]: berries susceptible until veraison; keep vines covered; combine with Lobesia spray.
- RULE: PM alerts = scouting-based ("first symptoms → treat; re-check weekly"); weather only modulates: favourable when daily mean 20–25 °C, RH 50–90 %, no leaf wetness; suppressed after days with ≥ 2 h at ≥ 35 °C (L. taurica); no weather-only "spray now".
- RISK: no validated weather model for greenhouse PM; TomatoPM needs canopy sensors and is field-calibrated.
